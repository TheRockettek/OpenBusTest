# Map configuration: incomplete support

Scope: `global.cfg`, tile `tile_<x>_<y>.map` records, terrain/ground-texture adjuncts, map scenery/attachments, Chrono variants, and road `.sli` profiles. OpenBus loads a useful static subset, but this is not full OMSI map behavior.

## Partial or unimplemented global/map records

| Keyword / data | Status | Current boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `[map]`, `[entrypoints]` | Active subset | Tile inventory and spawn selection work; duplicate tile rows are warned and aliased to the first tile. |
| `[groundtex]` and per-tile `.map.<index>.dds` | Partial | Base textures, per-tile alpha masks, and each record's repeated detail texture are rendered. Suffixes 1..N-1 map directly to non-base records; terminal suffix N maps to record N-1 for maps such as Cotterell. Seasonal selection and other source index conventions remain unverified. Some add-on `.dds` files contain BMP data; texture loading detects this by file signature. |
| Global metadata (`[description]`, `[NextIDCode]`, `[worldcoordinates]`, `[dynhelperactive]`, `[realrail]`, `[LHT]`, `[backgroundimage]`, `[mapcam]`, `[moneysystem]`, `[ticketpack]`, `[repair_time_min]`, `[years]`, `[realyearoffset]`, `[standarddepot]`, traffic-density records) | Partial runtime | Values/flags are retained. `[LHT]` determines the signed lane convention when a non-zero actor offset is requested; automatic traffic follows the authored path with no extra lateral offset. Road and passenger density points are sampled with provisional linear interpolation and endpoint clamping, then used as multipliers for separate capped AI-vehicle and human populations. Each defaults to 8, controlled by `OPENBUS_MAP_AI_COUNT` and `OPENBUS_MAP_HUMAN_COUNT` (0..200); `OPENBUS_MAP_AI_HOUR` (0..24) selects the schedule hour for both. Absent density data falls back to factor 1.0. Native density units/interpolation and the default time-of-day policy are unverified. Other unknown global sections are warned and preserved. |
| `ailists.cfg`, `drivers.txt`, `.hum` | Partial runtime | `[aigroup_2]` `.bus`/`.ovh` references and complete group payloads, `[aigroup_depot]` depot/HOF associations with linked bus-type `.bus` references and assignment payloads, and map-local `.hum` driver model paths are retained. Random road traffic uses only `.ovh` entries from the `NormalCars`, `Trucks`, and `Commercial`/`Commercials` groups; `.bus` vehicles are excluded and scheduled buses use bus-type `.bus` references on matched timetable routes. Buses wait for their parsed `.ttl` departure time before endpoint admission, including midnight rollover, and preserve the existing off-screen, distance, and clearance checks. If multiple pool buses serve a timetable route, its sorted departure list is partitioned across them so each departure has one owner. Both pools are capped at 200 actors. Vehicles follow directed lane offsets/heights and transformed scenery junction paths, joining compatible endpoints within 0.5 m horizontally and vertically. They stop at unconnected lane ends and continue forward through genuine loops; no artificial reverse traversal is added. Road vehicles move at a provisional 12 m/s, buses at 11 m/s. Human actors spawn from valid `drivers.txt` `.hum` configs on nearby human-path routes using both parsed `[humangeom]` dimensions, move at a provisional 1.2 m/s, and wrap at route ends. Active pedestrians are swept-footprint obstacles to road AI; pedestrians stop/resume when their route would overlap occupied vehicles or other active pedestrians. This is kinematic footprint avoidance, not physical collision response. Vehicle spawn, population activation/deactivation, and route-end events are logged. `OpenBusMapTrafficRuntimeProbe` covers repeated placement, directed-lane and junction traversal, loops, disconnected endpoints, departure clock rollover, non-duplicating departure partitioning, vehicle yielding to pedestrian footprints, and pedestrian stop/resume around vehicles. Human rendering currently uses colored primitive proxies with a simple distance-phased limb swing; `.hum` mesh/model configs, skeletal animation, and `[links]`, `[walk_param]`, `[voice]`, and `[mass]` runtime behavior are not implemented. |
| SCO `[traffic_lights_group]` / `[traffic_light]` / `[phase]` | Partial runtime | Fixed-cycle schedules are parsed and attached to paths through `[use_traffic_light]`. AI vehicles stop before linked paths for red, red/yellow, yellow, dark, invalid, or malformed schedules and proceed on phase states 6-8. Runtime regressions cover signalized route starts and approaches across loop wraps. `[approachdist]` and conditional `[traffic_light_stop]` / `[traffic_light_jump]` records are parsed, but request-driven timing is not simulated. Stop position is inferred from the linked path start; cycle origin and native stop-line fidelity are unverified. |
| AI road/pedestrian density transitions | Partial runtime | Preallocated AI vehicles and pedestrians are only hidden when off-screen; density increases admit actors only at clear off-screen entry positions. Inactive pedestrians advance invisibly on their routes while waiting for a safe admission point. Pool capacity remains fixed at startup, and native density semantics are unverified. |
| Tile `[terrain]` / `.map.terrain` | Partial | Static terrain sidecars are loaded and rendered. Dynamic terrain variants are not. A listed tile without a terrain sidecar has no terrain mesh. |
| `[water]` / `.map.water` | Provisional visual rendering | Each sidecar surface is rendered as a translucent, tile-wide quad with SW/SE/NE/NW corner heights. Corner order is reverse-engineered from neighboring installed OMSI maps; multiple surfaces per tile, exact material/opacity, and native OMSI visual parity are not verified. Water does not participate in vehicle collision. |
| `[variable_terrain]`, `[variable_terrainlightmap]` | Parsed only | Marker presence is reported; dynamic terrain/lightmap updates are not implemented. |
| `[rule]`, `[kill_rule]` | Parsed only | Four-field records are retained but do not hide, select, or otherwise affect placements. |
| `.map.LM.bmp`, `.map.terrain_0.rdy` | Inventoried only | Paths/presence are detected; payloads are not decoded/applied. |

## Tile placement and attachment records

| Keyword / family | Status | Current boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `[object]` | Partial | Static supported scenery placement is active. Counted placement labels feed label-oriented inline SCO text-texture slots, visible only on the authored front face; label/slot ordering is inferred and native OMSI visual parity remains unverified. Chrono overrides and some placement semantics are not implemented. |
| `[spline]`, `[spline_h]` | Partial | Road centerlines, elevation, cant, and a subset of profile rendering are active. Full OMSI geometry/material semantics and visual parity remain incomplete. |
| `[splineAttachement]` | Partial | Rows follow unique reciprocal spline links across tile boundaries. Missing/ambiguous IDs, invalid spline geometry, or chains without a usable local owner still produce no placement. |
| `[splineAttachement_repeater]` | Partial | Rows follow linked spline chains from their local owner; resolving the encoded repeater master tile/object index is still incomplete. |
| `[attachObj]` | Partial | Static parent-ID lookup and ordered `[new_attachment]` composition work across map tiles. Missing parents/anchors and cycles are skipped; script-variable behavior is not provided. |
| `[varparent]` | Parsed/validated only | Editor `Object Nr.` links are checked, but scenery script-variable inheritance is not implemented. This identifier is not treated as an attachment placement ID. |
| `[spline_terrain_align]` | Provisional | Marked scenery is tilted to the sampled terrain normal; exact OMSI behavior is unverified. |
| `[spline_terrain_align_2]` | Parsed only | The value is preserved but not applied to road/spline geometry. |
| Chrono `[selobject]`, `[selspline]`, `[delete]`, `[typ]`, `[relabel]` | Partial / not implemented | Selector IDs and raw tile content can be retained; date selection and variant application are not implemented. |

## Road profile `.sli` files

`[texture]`, `[profile]`, and `[profilepnt]` support a subset of static road-strip rendering, including straight and signed-radius centerlines. `.sli` `[path]` payloads retain their path-type code, lateral/height offsets, and direction (0 forward, 1 reverse, 2 bidirectional). Installed `invis_street.sli` and `invis_human.sli` use type codes `0` and `1` respectively; this inferred mapping separates road-vehicle and human route networks. Road vehicles use authored lane positions and directions rather than centerlines. Complete native OMSI traffic behavior is unverified; humans traverse type-1 routes but use proxy geometry with a simple walking-progress limb swing rather than configured `.hum` models. `[nocollision]` suppresses spline collision geometry without removing renderable sections. Known gaps include:

- Spline `skewStart` / `skewEnd` fields are applied as longitudinal shear per lateral distance in rendered/collision road geometry. At linked corners, when the authored endpoint axes already align (as in Cotterell splines 518/519 with approximately `+1`/`-1` skew), the seam now preserves that shared parallelogram axis instead of adding a second automatic miter. The exact native unit convention and broader OMSI visual parity remain unverified.
- `.sli` `[halfcantwidth]` explicitly sets cant clamping width; absent or invalid values fall back to valid `[heightprofile]` lateral extents. The four `[heightprofile]` fields are retained, but a complete authored height-profile surface is not implemented. Native visual parity remains unverified.
- Road markings, texture/material variations, chain continuity, curved cross-section seams, and full banking/cant parity still need validation.
- Editor-only profiles are intentionally omitted; profiles with missing texture files can render untextured.

## Not a keyword gap

A `.map` file listed in `global.cfg` but missing its `.terrain` sidecar is absent source data, not an unimplemented terrain keyword. Likewise, duplicate placement IDs and missing referenced textures/assets originate in the map/add-on data; the loader reports or skips them rather than rewriting the installed map. These cases should remain distinct from unsupported runtime semantics.

AI vehicles use directed authored lanes, not spline centerlines or artificial out-and-back routes. The runtime samples the same lateral/height offsets as F2 and includes the same transformed scenery junction paths. Lane ends join compatible starts within 0.5 m horizontally and vertically. Road vehicles make seeded choices among straight/left/right movements; generic route inspection uses straight-first selection. Timetable bus candidate selection checks up to 16 route seeds for a complete ordered spline sequence that is contiguous in either the timetable path or endpoint route, stopping early once all eligible timetable routes are matched. Per-vehicle minimum turning radii filter incompatible curves and discontinuous joins. Real loops continue forward, including routes that enter a loop after a one-way approach.

Only incoming lanes with no predecessor are spawn candidates. A preloaded vehicle pool queues at those endpoints, starting at distance zero and zero speed only when outside the main camera frustum, over 50 m plus its bounding radius from the camera, and clear of vehicle footprints. Closed circuits and internal spline joins are not spawn points. Density reductions defer removal until off-screen. At an unconnected route end, a vehicle stops and remains visible until that endpoint is outside the camera view and beyond the spawn-distance threshold; it then returns to the endpoint queue. A map may consequently run below its density target. Timetable-bus endpoint candidates are filtered using each bus model's minimum turning radius and do not fall back to mid-route spawns. `.ttl` departure minutes are linked to `.ttp` trips; matched buses wait until the next due departure before being admitted, including across midnight. If multiple pool buses serve a matched route, its departures are partitioned across them without duplicate assignment. Cotterell still has no complete endpoint-compatible timetable route, preventing live validation of this dispatch path there. Failure logs now include the best timetable line/destination, endpoint lane, seed, route lengths, offsets, and shared ordered spline IDs; the latest best candidate was U28 to University (112 timetable splines, 42 route splines, 16 shared splines from endpoint 129/lane 1/seed 5), first diverging at timetable/route spline IDs 10813/11507 after offsets 40/24. A timetable-directed retry at that junction is not enabled because it did not finish route reporting within 60 seconds; route exploration needs a tighter bound before another attempt.

The driving controller plans bend speed with a 1.8 m/s^2 lateral-acceleration limit, accelerates at up to 1.6 m/s^2, and normally brakes at up to 3 m/s^2. It uses a 1.5-second following target, a forward-only 2 m standstill buffer, current player/static-vehicle footprints, and conservative three-second crossing reservations. When a reservation blocks an approach, its wait age increases and is used to raise its priority over younger waiting movements, preventing permanent stable-order preference. This is a basic fairness policy, not authored right-of-way or full native signal behavior. Fixed-cycle SCO signal states are applied separately to linked route paths as described in the status table above. OBB sweeps in at most 0.05-second steps stop movement into occupied space, including during long frames; emergency blockage can require an immediate speed clamp. Vehicle dimensions come from configured bounding boxes; absent turning-radius data uses a class/wheelbase estimate. Polyline headings interpolate recovered tangents rather than jumping between tessellation chords. Routing backtracks from an infeasible later turn; if no drivable exit or loop exists, the entry route is rejected rather than inventing a disconnected end. Map load, queues, actual spawns, junction entry speeds, and unloads are logged.

[MapTrafficRuntimeProbe.cpp](../tests/MapTrafficRuntimeProbe.cpp) verifies incoming/reverse entry lanes, no closed-circuit spawns, hidden/clear entry admission, camera-frustum/near-distance spawn gating (front, behind, beside, edge-straddling, near, and beyond-far-plane entries), visible route-end retention and hidden route-end retirement, complete ordered route containment in either direction, rejection of partial/out-of-order route overlaps, actual left/right junction traversal, exact-radius acceptance and impossible-radius rejection, a speed below 4.5 m/s on 10 m turns, acceleration/braking bounds, stopped-player-bus avoidance without mutating the bus, slower-leader following, close-queue restart, long-frame blockage, adjacent-lane clearance, crossing separation/progress and wait-age priority, route backtracking, forward loops, scheduled-departure clock rollover, non-duplicating departure partitioning, signal-cycle wrap/invalid-schedule handling, and stopping/release across red/green signal phases. [SceneryObjectConfigProbe.cpp](../tests/SceneryObjectConfigProbe.cpp) verifies signal phase parsing, path association, approach distance, and conditional-record parsing. These are synthetic regressions, not proof of complete native driving parity. Cotterell startup checked 476 feasible endpoint routes across up to 16 seeds; 130 distinct spline IDs overlapped timetable paths, but the longest ordered contiguous match was 16 IDs, so no bus entry was accepted. A separate 220-second bounded run logged visible-end waits, off-screen endpoint unloads, and junction-path entries; it was stopped at the time limit rather than completing naturally, with no stderr recorded. This is log-based evidence, not visual driving validation. Resolving the Cotterell timetable/endpoint mismatch, full signal fidelity (conditional jump/stop behavior, cycle origin, and stop-line placement), sign compliance and authored right-of-way, fairness validation on representative live junctions, overtaking/lane changes, articulated swept-body dynamics, and physical collision response when the player drives into AI remain incomplete.
