# Map configuration: incomplete support

Scope: `global.cfg`, tile `tile_<x>_<y>.map` records, terrain/ground-texture adjuncts, map scenery/attachments, Chrono variants, and road `.sli` profiles. OpenBus loads a useful static subset, but this is not full OMSI map behavior.

## Real-world coordinate layout

Plain maps retain the 300 m grid. `[worldcoordinates]` maps use zoom-16 Web Mercator
rows counted north from the equator, with row-edge width
`(40075016.69 / 65536) / cosh(2 * pi * row / 65536)` metres. A tile's authored
horizontal span is the mean of its lower/upper edge widths; its height is the
upper edge width.

OpenBus uses the median tile row's mean width for a continuous, map-specific
runtime grid. Each tile's authored horizontal coordinates and mesh geometry are
scaled onto that grid separately in X and Y. Terrain, water, scenery, attachments,
road rendering/collision, traffic, entrypoints, culling, and streaming use the same
grid. Heights and authored spline distances remain in metres. This is a fixed-grid
approximation of the native camera-relative tile layout, not a geographic reprojection
of every mesh vertex or an exact replica of the original renderer.

Tile indices and large global runtime coordinates are retained; there is no origin
rebasing. Double-precision world calculations and tile-local float GPU road vertices
avoid losing road-edge detail at large northings. Nearby reciprocal road endpoints
still share a seam frame, reconciling small authored/projection discrepancies.
`OpenBusMapCoordinatesProbe` covers Bristol cross-row joins, projected traffic/scenery,
attachments, terrain/water, collision, inverse conversion, and GPU vertex precision.

With `OPENBUS_MAP_AI_SPAWN_DIAGNOSTICS=1`, road spawn admission logs report
`vehiclesAtTarget` for inactive vehicles whose road group (or shared road
population) is already full. Such vehicles do not scan spawn candidates.
`lowerPriority` counts candidate poses skipped after a better-scored clear candidate
was found; scoring combines player proximity, nearby traffic, SCO junction paths,
approaching signal stop lines, and a modest preference for network entry lanes.
`visible`, `obstructed`, and `clear` count checks actually performed,
not every candidate on the route. Search counters describe the most recently
consumed worker plan. Road and due scheduled-bus spawn searches run on the AI
worker using a snapshot; planned positions are reserved within each batch so
later proposals can select different clear positions. Proposals are revalidated on the main thread against
current population targets, tile, camera, player and clearance state before
admission. Scheduled-bus waiting diagnostics describe the worker snapshot.
Only one population plan is in flight, and the worker alternates
population planning with pending movement ticks. `MapTrafficWorker::populationPlan`
and `MapTrafficWorker::populationSpawnSearch` trace the offloaded work; planning
time also contributes to the worker's adaptive simulation-rate budget.

## Road profile `.sli` files

`[texture]`, `[profile]`, and `[profilepnt]` support a subset of static road-strip rendering, including straight and signed-radius centerlines. `.sli` `[path]` payloads retain their path-type code, lateral/height offsets, and direction (0 forward, 1 reverse, 2 bidirectional). Installed `invis_street.sli` and `invis_human.sli` use type codes `0` and `1` respectively; this inferred mapping separates road-vehicle and human route networks. Road vehicles use authored lane positions and directions rather than centerlines. Humans traverse type-1 routes and render configured `.hum` model meshes/textures; `.hum` and model CFG parsing, mesh parsing, and texture decoding run asynchronously, while OpenGL uploads remain on the render thread. Native walking animation and complete OMSI traffic behavior remain unverified. `[nocollision]` suppresses spline collision geometry without removing renderable sections. Known gaps include:

- Spline `skewStart` / `skewEnd` fields are applied as longitudinal shear per lateral distance in rendered/collision road geometry. At linked corners, when the authored endpoint axes already align (as in Cotterell splines 518/519 with approximately `+1`/`-1` skew), the seam now preserves that shared parallelogram axis instead of adding a second automatic miter. The exact native unit convention and broader OMSI visual parity remain unverified.
- `.sli` `[halfcantwidth]` explicitly sets cant clamping width; absent or invalid values fall back to valid `[heightprofile]` lateral extents. The four `[heightprofile]` fields are retained, but a complete authored height-profile surface is not implemented. Native visual parity remains unverified.
- Road markings, texture/material variations, chain continuity, curved cross-section seams, and full banking/cant parity still need validation.
- Editor-only profiles are intentionally omitted; profiles with missing texture files can render untextured.

## Not a keyword gap

A `.map` file listed in `global.cfg` but missing its `.terrain` sidecar is absent source data, not an unimplemented terrain keyword. Likewise, duplicate placement IDs and missing referenced textures/assets originate in the map/add-on data; the loader reports or skips them rather than rewriting the installed map. These cases should remain distinct from unsupported runtime semantics.

## Evidence and related pages

Runtime AI routes subdivide only long, straight, non-elevated spline segments. Cuts are at 10 m intervals, at least 5 m from either segment end; curved splines and point-based junction paths remain intact to avoid heading snaps and cuts near merges. Turn-radius checks and route spawn validation use the subdivided route; spawn checks treat contiguous pieces of an authored spline as one span. Traffic-rule priority follows OMSI's lower-value-wins convention; group-specific priority is matched to the actor's unscheduled group, and wait age orders only equal priorities.

Implemented AI traffic enhancements are summarized in
[TODO.md](../TODO.md#implemented-ai-traffic-enhancements); remaining work is
tracked in the [AI traffic implementation checklist](../TODO.md#ai-vehicle-and-human-traffic).

AI vehicles use directed authored lanes, not spline centerlines or artificial out-and-back routes. The runtime samples the same lateral/height offsets as F2 and includes the same transformed scenery junction paths. Lane ends join compatible starts within 0.5 m horizontally and vertically. Road vehicles make seeded choices among straight/left/right movements; generic route inspection uses straight-first selection. Timetable bus candidate selection first constrains directed-lane routing to ordered timetable spline sequences, then falls back to up to 16 seeded routes for a complete sequence contiguous in either the timetable path or endpoint route. Per-vehicle minimum turning radii filter incompatible curves and discontinuous joins. Real loops continue forward, including routes that enter a loop after a one-way approach.

Traffic arbitration does not require the whole remaining spline to be empty. Moving vehicles reserve a rolling, braking-aware local window; a held vehicle contributes its occupied footprint rather than an imagined future traversal. Distant queues on long scenery paths therefore do not hold incoming traffic at the authored path start. Signals clip reservations at their stop line, and priority yields are positioned at the nearest relevant geometric conflict. Compact junctions still check room to clear their box. The deliberate exception is an overlapping, opposing single-lane junction corridor where vehicles cannot pass: entry is exclusive until the admitted vehicle's rear clears the shared portion. This exception compares opposing path geometry, not spline IDs or total spline lengths, and a vehicle waiting at red before the corridor cannot claim it. Synthetic tests cover both signalized and unsignalized opposing corridors.

Junction admission also applies to closed and entry-loop routes; planning distances can extend across the loop seam. Tail-clearance queries select the actual junction segment touched by the vehicle instead of accidentally extending a departed junction's claim through its outgoing road spline. Regressions cover both loop representations and expiry of multi-connector claims.

Only incoming lanes with no predecessor are spawn candidates. A preloaded vehicle pool queues at those endpoints, starting at distance zero and zero speed only when outside the main camera frustum, over 50 m plus its bounding radius from the camera, and clear of vehicle footprints. Closed circuits and internal spline joins are not spawn points. Density reductions defer removal until off-screen. At an unconnected route end, a vehicle stops and remains visible until that endpoint is outside the camera view and beyond the spawn-distance threshold; it then returns to the endpoint queue. A map may consequently run below its density target. Timetable-bus endpoint candidates are filtered using each bus model's minimum turning radius and do not fall back to mid-route spawns. `.ttl` departure minutes are linked to `.ttp` trips; matched buses wait until the next due departure before being admitted, including across midnight. If multiple pool buses serve a matched route, its departures are partitioned across them without duplicate assignment. Candidate generation now constrains the directed lane search to timetable spline sequences when possible, while retaining the seeded search as fallback. Cotterell still has no complete endpoint-compatible timetable route, preventing live validation of this dispatch path there. Failure logs include the best timetable line/destination, endpoint lane, seed, route lengths, offsets, and shared ordered spline IDs; a previous best candidate was U28 to University (112 timetable splines, 42 route splines, 16 shared splines from endpoint 129/lane 1/seed 5), first diverging at timetable/route spline IDs 10813/11507 after offsets 40/24. Re-run this diagnostic against Cotterell to see whether timetable-guided routing resolves the mismatch.

The driving controller plans bend speed with a 1.8 m/s^2 lateral-acceleration limit, accelerates at up to 1.6 m/s^2, and normally brakes at up to 3 m/s^2. It uses a 1.5-second following target, a forward-only 2 m standstill buffer, current player/static-vehicle footprints, and conservative three-second crossing reservations. Authored path/group priority remains decisive before wait age. For equal-priority, uncommitted straight crossings, the vehicle approaching from the right receives a provisional preference until either actor has waited 3 seconds; then the existing turn/speed/arrival/wait tie-breakers resume. Other equal-priority movements use wait age for fairness. Closed, near-circular traffic loops are detected from their route geometry and receive maximum priority (256), overriding vehicle-group priority; approaching traffic does not inherit the priority merely because the roundabout lies ahead. This geometric heuristic and priority-preserving reservation policy are not a complete native OMSI right-of-way or signal model. Fixed-cycle SCO signal states are applied separately to linked route paths as described in the status table above. OBB sweeps in at most 0.05-second steps stop movement into occupied space, including during long frames; emergency blockage can require an immediate speed clamp. Vehicle dimensions come from configured bounding boxes; absent turning-radius data uses a class/wheelbase estimate. Polyline headings interpolate recovered tangents rather than jumping between tessellation chords. Routing backtracks from an infeasible later turn; if no drivable exit or loop exists, the entry route is rejected rather than inventing a disconnected end. Map load, queues, actual spawns, junction entry speeds, and unloads are logged.

[MapTrafficRuntimeProbe.cpp](../tests/MapTrafficRuntimeProbe.cpp) verifies connected-only entry selection, isolated/dead-end rejection, hidden/clear entry admission, camera-frustum/near-distance spawn gating (front, behind, beside, edge-straddling, near, and beyond-far-plane entries), immediate open-route retirement, complete ordered route containment in either direction, rejection of partial/out-of-order route overlaps, actual left/right junction traversal, exact-radius acceptance and impossible-radius rejection, a speed below 4.5 m/s on 10 m turns, acceleration/braking bounds, stopped-player-bus avoidance without mutating the bus, slower-leader following, close-queue restart, long-frame blockage, adjacent-lane clearance, crossing separation/progress, higher-priority precedence over an already-waiting lower-priority actor, route backtracking, forward loops, scheduled-departure clock rollover, non-duplicating departure partitioning, signal-cycle wrap/invalid-schedule handling, red/green/dark vehicle signal waiting, and authored pedestrian path offsets. [SceneryObjectConfigProbe.cpp](../tests/SceneryObjectConfigProbe.cpp) verifies signal phase parsing, path association, approach distance, zero-cycle fallback, and native-format stop/jump records. These are synthetic regressions, not proof of complete native driving parity. Cotterell startup checked 476 feasible endpoint routes across up to 16 seeds; 130 distinct spline IDs overlapped timetable paths, but the longest ordered contiguous match was 16 IDs, so no bus entry was accepted. A separate 220-second bounded run logged visible-end waits, off-screen endpoint unloads, and junction-path entries; it was stopped at the time limit rather than completing naturally, with no stderr recorded. This is log-based evidence, not visual driving validation. Resolving the Cotterell timetable/endpoint mismatch, native OMSI parity for conditional stop/jump timing and selected-time cycle origin, stop-line placement, sign compliance and authored right-of-way, fairness validation on representative live junctions, overtaking/lane changes, articulated swept-body dynamics, and physical collision response when the player drives into AI remain incomplete.
